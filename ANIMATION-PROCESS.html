The rule

Не кажи — покажи

No word appears in a graphic that the subtitle is already saying

Stronger than avoiding duplicates: the graphic contains no language at all. Language is the subtitle's job and it is already doing it. What the graphic owes the viewer is the thing the words can't deliver — a mechanism running, a quantity moving, a connection being made.

The test: mute the video and hide the subtitles. Can you still tell what this beat is about? If the answer needs the words, the graphic isn't finished.

Two exceptions, both narrow. Words inside a depicted interface are fine — a comment box with text being typed is showing an action, not captioning one. And a number is not a word: 1200 ₴ is a quantity, and quantities are exactly what type is for.

Pacing

A visual event every 3–5 seconds

Your delivery is slow and expert, not a barrage, and the cuts already give the eye somewhere to go. What they don't give is new information — a cut re-frames the same person saying the next sentence. So the beat rate sits on top of the cut rate, not instead of it.

For 51.5 seconds that's 11 to 17 events. Your reel 2 script produces eleven naturally, listed below — which means the structure is already there in the writing. Nothing has to be invented to hit the rate.

Composition

Where she goes, and why

Moving her is allowed and it should be used — but each of the four options means something different, and picking the wrong one is what makes an edit feel like flashing for its own sake.

Four positions · reel 2 usage
PositionUse whenBudgetCost
Overlay The graphic is an aside — it supports the sentence but isn't the sentence. 6 of 11 None. Default.
Behind her The graphic is context or environment — something happening around her, not to her. 1 of 11 No matting available, so it must live in frame margins she doesn't occupy — upper third only.
Split screen Two things must be seen at once, or she is reacting to the thing. 1 of 11 Strong move. Once per reel or it stops reading as special.
Fullscreen The graphic is the explanation for three seconds or more. 2 of 11 Removes her presence. Two per reel is the ceiling.

The constraint worth knowing: "behind her" cannot mean matting. Background removal runs at 3.2 seconds per frame on your machine — three hours for one reel. So anything behind her has to be composed into the space she isn't standing in, which for your framing means the upper third. That's a real limit on the idea, not a preference.

Process

The order to work in

  1. Read the script and mark every claim that has a shape

    Not every sentence — every claim with a quantity, a sequence, a connection, or a change of state. Those are the only things that can be shown. Aim for one every 3–5 seconds; if the script gives you fewer, the script is the problem, not the animation.

  2. For each one, write the verb before the noun

    Not "a chart of the cost" — "the cost falls to zero". Not "a diagram of the setup" — "a line connects the call to the tool". If you can't write a verb, you have a label, and labels are the subtitle's job.

  3. Delete every word from the graphic

    Then check what's left still reads. Numbers stay. Interface text stays. Everything else has to become a glyph, a shape, or a motion — or the beat gets cut.

  4. Choose the position, and justify it in one sentence

    Overlay, behind, split, or fullscreen — using the budget above. If the justification is "it looks better big", it's an overlay.

  5. Reuse an object rather than introducing a new one

    Three of reel 2's eleven beats are the same stack, and two are the same flowchart. A returning object carries everything it meant last time, so the second appearance costs nothing to read and lands harder.

  6. Put every element on its word

    Each branch of the flowchart fires on the word that names it. Uneven spacing is correct — evenly spaced elements look automated.

  7. Land it and leave it alone

    Once something has arrived it does not drift, pulse or breathe. The only exception in the whole reel is the loading ring at 15.30s, where the loop is the meaning.

  8. Watch it once at full speed, sound off, subtitles covered

    If you can follow the argument, it works. If you need the words back, go to step 2.
